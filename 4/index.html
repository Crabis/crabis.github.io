Part A.3: Warp the Images

In this part, I tested my computed homeographies on both a square and a rectangular object. As we can see, both the Nearest Neighbour and Bilinear interpolation methods work well, but the Bilinear method results in a smoother image with less sharp edges. However, the Bilinear method is more computationally intensive. For the purposes of the test images, it seems that Nearest Neighbour is sufficient. However, perhaps for higher quality images, Bilinear would be preferred.

Part A.4: Blend the Images into a Mosaic

In this part, I wanted to create a mosaic using 3 images. To achieve this, I used the one shot method to compute the homographies between the left-middle and middle-right image pairs, keeping the middle image as the reference frame. I then warped the left and right images into the middle image's frame of reference. Finally, I blended the three images together. However, trialling with the alpha mask to have a drop-off from the center to the edges did not work well, as it resulted in a very blurry image in the overlap regions. Instead, I chose to use a Winner Takes All approach and hybridized it with the alpha mask, creating a mask that only blended near the edges of the Winner Takes All regions. This helped me to produce much better results. I suspect it could also be a result of movement in my images, as the pictures in Yellowstone have geysers with moving steam.
